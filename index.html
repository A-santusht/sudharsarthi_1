📐 How the Platform Decides Issue Range & Jurisdiction

SUDHARSARTHI calculates geographic boundaries using three rigorous mathematical steps to ensure no overlapping contracts or wasted civic funds:
1. 25-Meter Spatial Radius
Uses the Haversine Great-Circle distance formula. Any new complaint within 25 meters of an existing defect is clustered as a repeat report rather than generating a duplicate ticket.
2. Defect Surface Area (sq.m)
AI vision scans citizen photos and calculates bounding length × width. A 1.2m × 0.8m crater = 0.96 sq.m, computing asphalt bitumen repair volume and accurate area measurement.
3. Ward Geofencing (6 Zones)
Duhai is partitioned into 6 municipal zones (Village, RRTS, North, South, East, West). GPS coordinates auto-route the alert to the nearest jurisdictional Junior Engineer.

⚖️ How the System Decides High, Medium, or Low Priority

🛣️ MANDATORY HIGHWAY PREFERENCE RULE:
All issues on NH-34, Delhi-Meerut Expressway, Meerut Road, and major flyovers are automatically classified as HIGH PREFERENCE. Because vehicles travel at 80–100 km/h, road defects pose fatal rollover and two-wheeler casualty risks. The system awards maximum traffic points (25/25) and enforces a strict <24 Hour SLA.
The 4-Factor Weighted Formula (0–100 Points):
Factor Weight Logic & Measurement
1. Structural Severity 0 to 40 Pts Defect type & depth. >10cm deep adds +8 to +16 pts; accident mentions add +8 pts.
2. Traffic Exposure 0 to 25 Pts Highway = 25 Pts (Forced High); Schools/Hospitals = +6 Pts; Quiet lanes = -4 Pts.
3. Repeat Complaints 0 to 20 Pts +7 points per unique nearby resident report or community upvote cluster.
4. Aging Factor 0 to 15 Pts +1.5 points per unaddressed day so neglected repairs naturally climb to High priority.
